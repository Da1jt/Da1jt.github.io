Civil aviation service: responsible disclosure

A 2024 access-control finding taught me that a security report is about the people behind the records, not just the flaw.

  1. 2024Identified the risk
  2. Private disclosureSubmitted through CNVD
  3. Documented outcomeCNVD record assigned

What I found

While examining a civil aviation mobile service, I found a weakness in how it controlled access to personal records. It raised the possibility that one account could see information belonging to another person. Unlike a competition challenge, this involved real users whose privacy depended on the system.

My role and the report

I investigated the behavior, documented the access-control risk, and submitted my findings through the China National Vulnerability Database (CNVD). The report received a CNVD record. That documents the submission; I have not independently confirmed a fix and make no claim about the service's current status.

Disclosure boundary

I name the sector, but not the product or organization. This public case study omits live addresses, requests, account identifiers, screenshots, and personal data; it contains no reproduction guide. Original evidence is reserved for appropriate private review, subject to the affected party's disclosure requirements.

What changed for me

Finding a weakness was only the beginning. Reporting it responsibly, protecting people's privacy, and helping others approach security work with the same care became the more important part of my work.
